> *"Desolation given physical form."*

**Black Ice** is a narcotic and arcane reagent born of the imprisonment of [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vharuk\|Vharuk]] — residue of the fallen god's unraveling essence, frozen into shards that never melt. A god of desolation does not get sealed away cleanly.

## What it is

Black ice is not ice in the normal sense — it is desolation given physical form. When Vharuk was bound beneath the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Doomlands\|Doomlands]] at the end of the [[rosvania/Wiki/War of Life\|War of Life]], the sheer weight of his imprisonment froze some of his own unraveling essence into shards of **matte-black, faintly warm-to-the-touch ice** that never fully melts — even in open flame.

It forms naturally in the frozen waters around the Doomlands and the islands of the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]]. What the maps mark with watchtower symbols is therefore more than a border patrol: the isles are a **containment line**, watching over a prison that keeps producing the one thing that proves what it holds.

## Effects

In small quantities, black ice is a **narcotic**. It numbs, then dulls the user's connection to fear, pain, and eventually empathy — exactly what one expects from something distilled out of a god whose entire domain is the absence of vitality.

In larger or purer doses — or with prolonged exposure — it begins to physically **unmake living tissue** at the edges: frostbite that does not stop, wounds that will not close, because the flesh has forgotten how to be alive.

## Why it is prized

Alchemists and dark cults alike treasure black ice for the same reason: it is one of the only substances in the world with a direct, uncorrupted line back to a [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Firstborn Wardens\|Firstborn Warden]]'s own essence. To touch it is, in a sense, to touch Vharuk.

## How it gets out

The [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]] were established to keep the Doomlands sealed and watched. The smuggling route that carries black ice south almost certainly means someone inside that watch chain is compromised — bribed, blackmailed, or already partially touched by the ice themselves.

The hook is best kept alive rather than resolved: the black market isn't *"criminals found a loophole"* — it's *"the containment is failing from within."* Quiet watchtowers that go dark, garrisons that report nothing wrong right up until they stop reporting at all — the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Silver Accord\|Silver Accord]] does not advertise these losses.

## Why Velmoria

A capital with a busy harbor, a wealthy merchant quarter (the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Bourgeosy\|City of Bourgeosy]]), and centuries of stability is exactly the kind of place a smuggling ring would want as its distribution hub:

- **Money** to buy discretion.
- **Traffic** enough to hide shipments in the docks.
- A population **complacent** enough after the Still Years that nobody looks too hard for gods' blood on ice.

## A note on concealment

Some theologians whisper that the route has stayed unnoticed this long because concealment itself is the domain of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Serathis\|Serathis]] — and concealment does not discriminate about who it protects. The Shadowbinder does not serve Vharuk; the veil simply does not care whose secret it keeps.

## Origins in the Cracking Age

The trade very plausibly first takes root in the Cracking Age (1301–1459 SA): a fresh, chaotic border, a newly formed watch order still finding its footing, and a prison cracking open for the first time in centuries. Corruption finds its easiest foothold in institutions too young to have built up their own discipline.
