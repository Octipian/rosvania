# The Chronicles of Rosvania

*Being a history of the world, from the first silence to the present shadow.*

---

## Prologue: The Making of the World

Before dawn or memory, before the breath of wind or the stirring of stars, there was only the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Unseen Father\|Unseen Father]] — nameless and eternal, the source from which all light and law were born. He looked upon the vast nothing and desired harmony, and so He uttered Three Decrees, shaping the silence into song.

By the First Decree, earth was divided from sky. Mountains rose like crowns of stone, and the depths were filled with water — thus were formed the lands of Rosvania and the seas that bound them. By the Second Decree, the Father set the sun and stars in their slow dance, and from their fire came warmth, rhythm, and time. By the Third Decree, He breathed upon the clay of the world, and life took root. Trees learned to whisper. Beasts learned to roam. And at last the elder races awoke — the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Elves\|Elves]] from starlight, the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Dwarves\|Dwarves]] from the mountain's heart, and [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Men\|Men]] from the soil between them.

Thus was born the first harmony: a kingdom of light and silence, ruled unseen by the Father's will.

To tend this creation, the Father drew from His own essence eight divine spirits — the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Firstborn Wardens\|Firstborn Wardens]], radiant in power and purpose. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Solareth\|Solareth]], the Sun's Flame, Warden of Valor and Dawn, whose light banished shadow. [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Namarielle\|Namarielle]], the Lifebringer, Mother of Renewal, who taught the forests to breathe. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Korran-Dur\|Korran-Dur]], the Deep Warden, Father of Stone, guardian of the Dwarves and the hidden forges. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Vael'Thara\|Vael'Thara]], the Star-Singer, whose voice shaped the dreams of mortals into grace. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Erynos\|Erynos]], the Tidebreaker, Lord of the Seas, whose wrath and mercy flowed as one. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Serathis\|Serathis]], the Shadowbinder, Keeper of Secrets, veiling what must remain unseen. [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Athera\|Athera]], the Fateweaver, who alone glimpsed the threads of destiny. And [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vharuk\|Vharuk]], the Stillborn Flame, Warden of Silence and Rest, who cherished the unbroken calm above all things.

Under their care, creation flourished. The stars sang. The rivers gleamed. And for a time, the Father's kingdom stood perfect in its balance.

---

## Chapter I: The Pride of Vharuk

But pride can be born even in stillness.

Vharuk, Warden of Silence, looked upon mortal life and found it wanting. Their laughter, their grief, their fleeting passions — all of it disturbed the perfect hush He so loved. In the cold vault of His heart, admiration curdled first into disdain, and disdain hardened at last into resolve: that silence alone was purity, and that life itself was nothing but a flaw to be corrected.

While His kindred nurtured what the Father had made, Vharuk labored in secret, twisting beauty into mockery. From the great wolves and serpents of the mountains, He forged the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Orcs\|Orcs]] — creatures of fire and fury, made in the shape of conquest. From the crawling things of the deep earth, He shaped the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Goblins\|Goblins]] — small, covetous things that would learn to thrive on the ruin of others. He called this work the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Doctrine of Desolation\|Doctrine of Desolation]]: the belief that the world must be emptied of life before it could ever return to true perfection.

For a time, His treachery went unseen. But treachery, like silence, cannot hold forever.

---

## Chapter II: The War of Life

At last Vharuk's defiance could no longer be hidden. He had gone further even than the forging of monsters — He had stolen from Namarielle the breath of renewal itself and twisted it into something obscene, and from that theft rose the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/The Unmade\|Unmade]]: beings that were neither living nor dead, perfect only in their hollow stillness.

When the other Wardens learned what He had done, the heavens themselves trembled. Vharuk did not deny it. He declared open war upon all life, proclaiming — with the terrible sincerity of the truly convinced — that to cleanse the world was to save it.

The Unseen Father, stricken with grief beyond words, withdrew from His own creation. He decreed only this: that His children must now defend, with their own strength, what He had given them.

So began the [[rosvania/Wiki/War of Life\|War of Life]] — a war fought not between kingdoms but between gods, with mortals swept up in its wake like leaves before a storm. Solareth gathered the banners of the living into the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Alliance of Life\|Alliance of Life]]: the Elves, guided by Vael'Thara's unbroken song; the Dwarves, armored in light-forged steel drawn from Korran-Dur's own forges; and the Men of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Tuzland and the Whispering Sands\|Tuzland]], who though they sent only a small host, sent their greatest — the **Demigods of the Hammered Sun**, led by [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Tuzlak the Great King\|Tuzlak the Great King]] and the mighty [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/The Big G\|Big G]].

The battles of that age split mountains and scorched the stars. Vharuk's own children — the Orc hordes and Goblin tribes — flocked to His banner and fought beside the divine Unmade, and for a long, terrible while, it was not at all certain that life would win.

But at last the hosts of the living came to the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Black Spire of Mourning\|Black Spire of Mourning]], Vharuk's fortress in the far north, and there Solareth struck the blow that ended the war. Vharuk's body shattered beneath it — but a god's essence cannot be slain so easily, only bound. It fell to [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Athera\|Athera]] and [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Namarielle\|Namarielle]] to seal what remained of Him beneath the world, in a prison of eternal ice that the ages since have called the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Doomlands\|Doomlands]].

So ended the [[rosvania/Wiki/War of Life\|War of Life]]. The world was saved. But it would never again be whole — and something of that cost never quite left the Wardens who paid it. Solareth's light has never burned as bright again. Namarielle's essence, spent in the binding, left a wound in her own domain that has not healed even now. Korran-Dur withdrew so deep into the stone that even His own Dwarves cannot follow Him there, though they still feel His hand in every forge. Vael'Thara never fully left at all — some say Her voice still lives in the song the deep forests sing. Erynos alone remained willing to walk among mortals when His wrath demanded it. Of Serathis, almost nothing is said even now; concealment, after all, does not announce itself. And Athera's part in the sealing left Her mark on certain quiet, half-forgotten places of the world — grounds sacred to wild and moonlit things, long before any kingdom rose to claim them.

---

## Chapter III: The First Age — Gods and Empires

### The Rebuilding

When the war ended, the Wardens fell silent, and mortals were left to stand alone among the ashes of gods. Vharuk's broken children scattered to the world's harshest corners — the Orcs to the black plains of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Gorthul-Maar\|Gorthul-Maar]] in the northeast, the Goblins to the burning [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Emberwastes\|Emberwastes]] in the south. What began as shattered, leaderless warbands hardened slowly into something more — for in a land as unforgiving as Gorthul-Maar, only the chieftains who could truly unite their kin survived at all. It was from that hard schooling that a warlord not yet born would one day inherit the strength to unite them all.

Out of the ruin, Old Tuzland grew strong again, builders and kings rising where there had been only ash. And east of Tuzland's plains, upon the memory of an older Menor, the first true human empire began to rise once more.

### The Age of Empire and Shadow

With the gods silent, mortal pride found room to grow. The human lands claimed the mantle of Solareth Himself, unifying as the [[Solar Empire of Menor\|Solar Empire of Menor]], and turned their strength first against the Orcs of Gorthul-Maar, then — to fund the war — against the Goblins of the Emberwastes, driving them from the only home they had known into the forests of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Vael'nore\|Vael'nore]]. Stripped of land and unable to match Menor in open war, the Goblins turned instead to something crueler and far more lasting. Under a chieftain remembered only as **Grak the Chain-Master**, they built the first [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Goblin Syndicates\|Syndicates]] — an economy built not on what the Goblins could grow or forge, but on what they could sell of other people's suffering. It would prove to be the longest-lived institution any Goblin ever built.

It was in this same proud age that the gravest deceit since Vharuk's own took root. Deep beneath the roots of Etharion, a fragment of Vharuk's broken mind stirred — a thing the old records call only the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Whisperer Beneath the Roots\|Whisperer Beneath the Roots]]. Cloaked in false radiance, it found Queen [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Vael'Nir\|Vael'Nir]] of the Elves and filled her with promises of Solareth's glory returned. Under its spell, Etharion became **Elarith**, the Radiant Dominion, and marched to holy war against Men. The [[rosvania/Wiki/War of the Radiant Blades\|War of the Radiant Blades]] burned for two decades before the lie broke under the weight of its own cruelty — the more the Elves saw of what they had become, the weaker the Whisperer's hold grew, until at last it was burned away entirely in fires of grief and shame. Vael'Nir fell, and Etharion renounced divine worship altogether, choosing thereafter to be ruled by an Archmage Council rather than any crown that might again be so easily deceived.

### The Shattered Sea

Repentance, it seems, does not always outlast greed for long. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Tuzland and the Whispering Sands\|Tuzland]] and Menor together turned their eyes upon the ocean itself, warring against the Aquatic Empires for dominion of the sea. [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Erynos\|Erynos]], Lord of the Tides, had not forgotten the arrogance of mortals, and He answered with a wrath the world had not seen since the War of Life. A tidal doom swallowed half of Tuzland's coast whole, drowning cities and leaving behind only the pale dunes now called the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Tuzland and the Whispering Sands\|Whispering Sands]]. The Solar Empire fell with it, its capital swallowed by the returning wave.

Into that ruin marched [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Khar'doth the Uniter\|Khar'doth the Uniter]] — the first Orc warlord in living memory to bind every clan of Gorthul-Maar beneath a single banner, rather than the scattered few his forebears had managed. He found not a defended empire waiting for him but a broken, grieving people with nothing left to resist him. So the [[rosvania/Wiki/Eras & Timeline/First Age\|First Age]] ended: in salt, in silence, and in the war-cries of Orcs marching where the songs of Men once rose.

## Chapter IV: The Second Age — Mortals Alone

### The Years of Ash

For three centuries the world knew nothing but hunger and exile. Khar'doth's Orcs ruled the eastern plains unchallenged, the Goblin Syndicates grew fat on chains and misery, and humanity wandered without direction, too broken by Menor's fall to even properly grieve it.

Yet even in this darkest of centuries, something new was born. Refugees fleeing the drowned ruins of Menor and the ashes of Tuzland pushed west into a marshland nobody else wanted, and there they founded a small kingdom of their own — [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Rizzhia\|Rizzhia]], under the line of **Sh'rek**, living quietly on fish, reed-cloth, and amber while the rest of the world was too broken to notice them at all. It did not last. Around the two-hundred-ninetieth year of the age, a lesser Orc chieftain laid siege to Rizzhia's borders, and though the marsh-folk held out, plague broke out in both camps before the siege could end in blood. Rizzhia did not fall to the sword. It fell to sickness, and its people ended in the same mass graves as the very Orcs who had come to conquer them — the first nation of the new age to rise and vanish, gone before the world had even begun to remember how to rebuild.

It was in that grave that something far older than Rizzhia itself finally stirred. A splinter of Vharuk's own Unmade, buried since the War of Life and long forgotten, woke amid the rot and rose as the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Necrotic King\|Necrotic King]] — no ghost of Rizzhia, but an ancient wound in the world that Rizzhia's dying had simply uncovered. What was once a kingdom became the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Swamp of Sh'rek\|Swamp of Sh'Rek]], and has remained so ever since.

### The Forgeborn Era

Fire found the ashes again all at once, as if the world itself had been waiting. In the southwest, a chieftain named [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Taner\|Taner]] began uniting the scattered valleys of Tuzland into what would become the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Tuzlean Concord\|Tuzlean Concord]]. In that very same year, upon a nameless harbor on the western coast, a claimant of fallen Menor's blood — **Duke Aurelian** — planted his banner and founded [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Velmoria\|Velmoria]], the seed of an empire not yet imagined. East, among the ruins of old Menor, scholars rediscovered the lost art of refining Solarite, and armed with it, Men began at last to push the Orcs of Gorthul-Maar back north, beyond the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Silver Marches\|Silver Marches]], into the cold they had come from.

### The War of Bloodlines

Victory, as it so often does, curdled. Within Tuzland, the noble houses turned blade against blade, and the [[rosvania/Wiki/Tuzlean Civil War\|Tuzlean Civil War]] consumed the land for the better part of three centuries. From that long ruin rose [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Taner IV\|Taner IV]], last of the half-divine commanders — a relic of an age when gods still whispered to mortal kings — and when he died, what little unity remained died with him. His surviving captains took to the sea rather than serve any lesser heir, and so were born the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Tuzlean Corsairs\|Tuzlean Corsairs]], black sails haunting the coast for generations after. Out of the same collapse, a band of warriors who trusted no crown at all declared their independence and founded the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Hold of Five Trials\|Hold of Five Trials]], a land ruled by oath rather than blood.

Strangely — or perhaps not so strangely — this same age of collapse found Velmoria too. The ducal line that Duke Aurelian had founded lost its grip in these very centuries, and the city fractured into rival houses even as Tuzland tore itself apart an ocean away. Whether this was mere coincidence, or the first faint tremor of something already stirring beneath the world's calm, no chronicler has ever agreed.

### The Quiet Reign

At last, a weary stillness settled over the land. **Emperor Aerion** ended Velmoria's long fracture, crowning himself rather than settling for a duke's title, and renamed his reunified realm the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Aurelian Dominion\|Aurelian Domain]]. Under his successor, **Emperor Solric**, that Domain entered a golden age the like of which the Second Age had not yet seen — the **Palace of the Sun** rose in Velmoria, and around it the wealthy quarter men would come to call the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Bourgeosy\|City of Bourgeosy]], and beside them both, the quiet halls of the **Sanctum of Zerzabal**.

It was in this same flush of prosperity that a prospector named **Cassian Vareth**, granted rights to the iron and rare fire-gold of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Ashrend Peaks\|Ashrend Peak]], raised a mining camp that would soon be chartered as a proper city — [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Ruins of Varethil\|Varethil]], named for its founder, and in time home to some four thousand souls of every kindred who traveled that southern road. And in the shadows beneath every golden age, the Goblin Syndicates found their own prosperity too, as a lanista remembered as **Batrok** filled the arenas of a continent grown rich enough to afford spectacle with gladiators bred and sold like any other good.

### The Cracking Age

But beneath the quiet, something had already begun to stir. The seals that Athera and Namarielle had forged around Vharuk's prison, unbroken since the War of Life, began at last to weaken. Tremors reached the Aurelian court in whispers before they reached it in truth — that Vharuk's prison no longer slept so soundly. Expeditions sent north to learn the truth simply vanished, swallowed by whatever waited in the ice.

Sensing weakness where there had only ever been silence, a new Orc warlord — [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Warlord Gorgath\|Gorgath]] — rose in Gorthul-Maar and began striking deep into Aurelian lands, testing walls that had held for centuries. Against this rising dread, the frontier's holdfasts, its half-elven marcher lords, and the Aurelian knights sent to relieve them did the only thing that made sense: they stopped fighting as strangers and began fighting as one. So was born the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Silver Accord\|Silver Accord]], sworn in silver ink and silver coin — a metal already known to ward against corruption — under a young half-elven commander named **Eltharion**. Within the Accord's ranks, the old scattered watch over the Doomlands found new purpose and a new name: the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]], one order with two fronts, holding both an Orc border and a god's prison in the same breath.

It was here, too — in an order still too young to have learned its own discipline — that the world's darkest secret first slipped south along the Accord's own watch-lines: shards of Vharuk's own essence, frozen in his imprisonment, smuggled out as [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Black Ice\|black ice]]. In small measure it dulls fear and pain until it dulls the soul entirely; in greater measure, it unmakes living flesh the way its source once dreamed of unmaking the world. No one now living remembers who first thought to sell it. Someone, somewhere along that young and unweathered watch, simply did.

### The Still Years

Forty winters passed that felt like peace but were not. [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Emperor Taryon V\|Emperor Taryon V]] ruled a Domain that called the northern omens superstition and the southern raids a nuisance to be managed rather than a warning to be heeded. In one of those quiet years — the eighty-seventh of the fifteen-hundredth century, if the old dating holds — a dragon came to [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Ashrend Peaks\|Ashrend Peak]] to give birth, and the people of Varethil, as frightened folk have always done, marched up the mountain to drive it away. They succeeded. The cost was the city itself: the dragon returned over Varethil on its way out, wounded and past reason, and what its fire did not take, the collapsing mine-tunnels beneath the burning streets did. A third of the city survived. It was never rebuilt.

And through it all, unseen by any court, walked [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vorun Khar\|Vorun Khar]] — a mortal fragment of Vharuk's own will, wearing the patient face of a wanderer and calling himself, when he was noticed at all, the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vorun Khar\|Hidden Witness]]. He let Gorgath's raids hold the Emperor's attention, and while kings worried over a border, he moved freely among them, measuring the hearts of mortals for a Desolation not yet come. In these Still Years, even false light could pass, for a while, as hope.

## Chapter V: The Third Age — Shadow and Return

### The Breaking of the Seal

The Still Years did not end with thunder. They ended with a whisper — a sound like distant ice fracturing across an eternity that had, until then, seemed unbreakable. None can say exactly when the first Seal of the Doomlands gave way. Only that the stars began to burn a little dimmer, and that the wind from the north began, for the first time in a thousand years, to carry the smell of death.

### Year One — The Chosen Party

On the road to [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Arglaxx\|Arglaxx]], amid a goblin ambush that should have meant nothing, four strangers found themselves fighting side by side: [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Floris Flex\|Floris]], a scholar of forbidden arts; [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Gerard\|Gerard]], a sellsword who trusted his blade more than his judgment; [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Kazzimir\|Kazzimir]], a warrior whose scars spoke of things beyond mortal wounding; and [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Levren\|Levren]], who said little and revealed less. The skirmish ended not by their hand but by the shadow of [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Golgoth\|Golgoth]], last of the elder drakes, whose arrival silenced the field entirely.

Golgoth named them bound — the first Chosen of a new and darkening age — and set them a single task: recover an amulet lost since the old wars, said to grant its bearer's truest desire, or else unmake them for having wanted it. What followed was a road strange even by the standards of a world waking to shadow: a [[rosvania/Wiki/Artifacts & Items/Cursed Map\|cursed map]] that moved like living ink; a mage who vanished into the night rather than speak what he had seen in it; an illusion of Golgoth himself, fed unwittingly stronger by Kazzimir's own healing hand; and at last, an ancient shrine that judged them worthy at the **Trial of Will**, though not without first testing what little trust bound them together.

### Year Two — What the Swamp Remembered

Floris left them at the dawn of the new year, his pride grown too heavy to carry alongside the others. The three who remained — Kazzimir, Gerard, and Levren — soon met a girl marked by plague, from Kazzimir's own home in the Swamp of Sh'Rek. She led them back toward the mire, through an ambush at [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Ashrend Peaks\|Ashrend Peak]] gone wrong, a vengeful horde and a mountain troll, an Eldritch Tree Guardian drawn by the noise of it all, and at last to [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Lotusden\|Lotusden]], city of crimson lanterns, where a silver-tongued blacksmith sold them a [[rosvania/Wiki/Artifacts & Items/Deck of Some Things\|cursed and unpredictable deck of fate]] itself.

At the swamp, the plague-girl showed her true face at last: a vessel for the [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Necrotic King\|Necrotic King]], waked once more from the old grave beneath the mire. The battle that followed tore the Swamp's stillness apart and raised Rizzhia's ancient dead with it. When the water finally went quiet again, something vast and black-limbed rose from beneath it and dragged a vengeful, summoned enemy down into the dark before it could strike — a mercy from no one the party could name.

They returned at last to Kazzimir's home in [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Zaporizzia\|Zaporizzia]], where the same hollow-eyed wizard who once studied their cursed map found them again, his voice trembling with what he now knew for certain. *Vharuk wakes again,* he told them. *The Dreaming God stirs beneath the veil. You must finish the Trials. Only then will the Protectors of the Realm accept you — and only then may the world stand a chance.*

### Year Three — The Present

From Zaporizzia they sought passage across the sea, and found it only in a drunken captain named [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Salazar\|Salazar]] and his battered brigantine, the *[[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Good ol' Jacky\|Good ol' Jacky]]* — cheap, miserable, and somehow unsinkable. They needed that last quality sooner than they expected. A Tuzlan Corsair ambush in the fog was cut short when [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Captain Serak\|Captain Serak, the Leviathan Caller]], rose from the deep aboard his spectral ship and opened fire on friend and foe alike. Kazzimir, shifted into a great octopus to fight beneath the waves, found his own mind seized and turned against his companions — and still the cursed *Jacky* refused to go down.

Then the sky itself split open, and [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Erynos\|Erynos]] came. Serak fled rather than challenge a god outright; a Tuzlan fleet of fifty ships, crying of holy reconquest, was not so wise, and Erynos ended them all in a single moment of wrath. From the wreckage the party pulled a swirling, cursed orb — a vision so vast it locked Kazzimir's mind in place until Salazar, in the only way he knew, simply smashed it to pieces.

At the [[rosvania/Wiki/Sessions & Campaign/Session 3 - The Trial of Combat\|Trial of Combat]] at last, judged by [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vorlag\|Vorlag]], Commander of the Protectors of the Realm, by [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Brother Tarsis\|Brother Tarsis]], a dwarven priest bearing a hammer forged from the Deep's own Gift, and by a singer named [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Josephine Kane\|Josephine Kane]] whose presence there no one has explained, the party fought and won a brutal tournament, and the path to the next Trial opened before them.

Somewhere north of all of it, the Breath of Vharuk still crawls a little further south with every season that passes. It has not stopped since the seal first cracked. It shows no sign of stopping now.

## Appendix: Houses and Thrones

**Tuzland** — [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Tuzlak the Great King\|Tuzlak the Great King]] and the [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/The Big G\|Big G]] (Demigods of the Hammered Sun, fell in the [[rosvania/Wiki/War of Life\|War of Life]]) → [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Taner\|Taner I]]–[[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Taner IV\|IV]] (Second Age unifiers, last of the half-divine blood) → the noble houses, fractured in civil war → the pirate captains of the coast and the warrior lords of the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Hold of Five Trials\|Hold of Five Trials]], who answer to no throne at all.

**Menor / the Solar Empire** — Tharion I, who founded it → Malryc I, who grew it → Malryc II, who drowned with it.

**Etharion** — guided by no throne but by archmages, until Queen [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Vael'Nir\|Vael'Nir]] was corrupted and fell in the [[rosvania/Wiki/War of the Radiant Blades\|War of the Radiant Blades]] → ruled since by an Archmage Council that has sworn never again to trust divine promise unquestioned.

**Durakheim** — an unbroken line beneath the mountain: Korradin the Mortal Saviour, then Durak I, then Durak II, then Balin the Usurper, then Thurrig, then Durak III, who reigns still.

**The Aurelian Domain** — Duke Aurelian, who raised [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Velmoria\|Velmoria]] from a nameless harbor → the ducal line, four centuries strong before it broke → Emperor Aerion, who mended it → Emperor Solric, whose golden age still gilds the capital → Emperor Aurelian V, who marched north → [[rosvania/Wiki/Rulers/Emperor Taryon V\|Emperor Taryon V]], who rules still, uncertain how much longer the quiet can hold.

**The Silver Accord and the Protectors of the Realm** — Grand Marshal Eltharion, who forged the pact in 1301 and may, if the old tales of half-elven years are true, be leading it even now, two centuries on.

**Rizzhia** — the line of Sh'rek, marsh-kings of a kingdom that no longer exists, remembered now only by the swamp that wears their name.

**Gorthul-Maar** — [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Khar'doth the Uniter\|Khar'doth the Uniter]], who first bound every clan beneath one banner → [[rosvania/Wiki/Characters/Warlord Gorgath\|Warlord Gorgath]], who tests the Aurelian border even now.

**Vharuk** — bound beneath the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Doomlands\|Doomlands]] since the tenth year after His fall, and yet not entirely absent: [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vorun Khar\|Vorun Khar]], His mortal fragment, has walked among the living since the world was young, and walks among them still, unseen and unproven, waiting for the silence He has always wanted.
