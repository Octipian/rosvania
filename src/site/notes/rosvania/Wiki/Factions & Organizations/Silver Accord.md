> *"It wasn't founded in a golden age. It was founded because everyone who lived there had already lost something."*

The **Silver Accord** is the frontier pact of the northern marchland — a standing crusader state born from a shared wound, and structurally the only thing between the world and [[rosvania/Wiki/Deities & Gods/Vharuk\|Vharuk]]'s prison failing. Its ruler-list bundles it with the **[[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]]** because they are not two nations but one order with two fronts: land and sea, Orcs and gods.

> [!note] A correction to older readings
> Earlier accounts described the Silver Accord as an eastern trading confederation. The Accord is the marchland frontier state between [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Etharion\|Etharion]], [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Durakheim\|Durakheim]], and [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Gorthul-Maar\|Gorthul-Maar]] — the eastern markets of [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Vael'nore\|Vael'nore]] and the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Silver Marches\|Silver Marches]] are not its territory.

## What the land was before

Long before it had a name of its own, the stretch of highland and forest wedged between Etharion, Durakheim, and Gorthul-Maar was nobody's country — a marchland squeezed by three peoples who all had better reasons to look elsewhere.

- To the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Elves\|Elves]] of Etharion it was the wild edge beyond their borders, unworthy of settlement.
- To the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Dwarves\|Dwarves]] of Durakheim it was surface land, and Durakheim has never cared much for the surface.
- To the [[rosvania/Wiki/Races/Orcs\|Orcs]] of Gorthul-Maar it was simply raiding ground — close enough to strike into, far enough that nothing held it against them.

For most of the [[rosvania/Wiki/Eras & Timeline/First Age\|First Age]] and the early centuries of the [[rosvania/Wiki/Eras & Timeline/Second Age\|Second Age]], that is all it was: a bleeding edge. **Half-elven refugees** — children of Etharion's wars, unwelcome on either side of their own bloodline — drifted into it because nowhere else wanted them either. **Human frontier families**, pushed out of Tuzland's civil war (690–974 SA) or the Aurelian duchy's own fracture in that same dark stretch, filtered in looking for land nobody else had claimed. What grew there was not a nation: scattered holdfasts, marcher lords, and knightly bands who owed nothing to any crown, held together by nothing but the fact that Gorthul-Maar's raiders did not care whose farm they burned.

## The founding crisis — 1300 SA

Something changed in 1300 SA. The histories do not agree on a single cause — and that is deliberate; the truth is a thread left open. Three things are certain, and they happened close enough together to look connected:

- **Raids out of Gorthul-Maar sharpened** from opportunistic to organized — something was pushing the Orcs to move as one force through **Kor-gul Pass** rather than as scattered warbands.
- **The northern watch broke containment.** The watch along the frozen islands guarding the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Doomlands\|Doomlands]] — the chain later called the [[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]] — reported the first confirmed breach in generations. Something had gotten out, or had gotten in to buy it.
- **The Northern Expeditions began.** In the same year, Emperor **Aurelian V** of the [[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Aurelian Dominion\|Aurelian Dominion]] sent soldiers north. Lined up against the dates, they were not expeditions of conquest at all: they were a response. The marchland's holdfasts had sent word south that they could not hold the line alone.

Whether these three things shared one true cause — a stirring of Vharuk himself, a deliberate provocation, the first traces of what would later surface as the **[[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Black Ice\|Black Ice]]** trade — is a thread best left for the table to answer. It is too good a hook to spend now.

## The founding — 1301 SA

What came out of that crisis was the **Silver Accord**: a formal pact between the marchland's scattered holdfasts, its half-elven marcher lords, Aurelian's expeditionary knights, and — critically — surviving orders sworn to the old watch over the Doomlands. They did not unite because they liked each other. They united because 1300 SA proved none of them could survive alone, and because whatever had happened at the Protectors made it clear the north needed watching permanently — not just when a crisis forced attention that way.

The name comes from the treaty's sealing, signed by tradition with **silver ink and silver coin** — silver being already known to the old watch-orders as a ward against corruption and the touch of things that should not still be moving. A symbolic choice became a literal one: silver became the Accord's signature metal in arms and armor — not decoration but doctrine, worn by knights who expected, eventually, to fight something ordinary steel would not stop.

## Grand Marshal Eltharion

**Grand Marshal Eltharion** — a half-elf, likely one of the marchland's own refugee-born marcher lords risen to lead the founding coalition — became the Accord's first commander. His name carries the whole two centuries of the Accord's ruler-list (1301–1501 SA) not because the office never changed hands, but almost certainly because **he simply hasn't died**: a half-elf's longer lifespan turning "Grand Marshal Eltharion" from a man into something closer to an institution. Two centuries into the role, he may well be the single oldest living authority figure on the map who was there for a nation's founding — and is, arguably, still in charge of it.

## Two duties, one oath

The Accord organized itself around two duties that never separated:

- **The land war.** Its crusader knights and half-elven guardians held the line against Gorthul-Maar, garrisoning towns like Northern and Southern Valenshar, Grailah Town, Ardenvalle, and Wyanisy — the line of settlements running along the Orc border, with Kor-gul Pass and the blighted region of Bloodmesa marking where the true fighting happens. This is not a peaceful nation. It is a standing frontier army that happens to also farm, trade, and raise children.
- **The northern watch.** The Accord absorbed and formalized the old watch over the Doomlands, giving the scattered island fortresses their present name: the **[[rosvania/Wiki/Factions & Organizations/Protectors of the Realm\|Protectors of the Realm]]**. A knight of the Accord might spend a decade on the Kor-gul line and the next posted to a frozen watchtower staring at the Doomlands — and it is the same oath either way.

This is the weight no other nation on the map carries: the Accord is, structurally, the only thing standing between the world and Vharuk's prison failing. Everyone else gets to treat the Doomlands as a distant myth. The Accord does not have that luxury.

## Two centuries of war nobody wins cleanly

The years 1301–1501 SA — the same stretch the maps date themselves to, the "[[rosvania/Wiki/Eras & Timeline/Second Age#1460–1501 SA — The Still Years\|Still Years]]" — are anything but still for the Accord, even while the rest of the continent stagnates. A frontier does not get to stagnate:

- **Repeated Orc surges** out of Gorthul-Maar — none as coordinated as 1300 SA's, but frequent enough that towns like the Fort of Ston exist purely as hardened strongpoints rather than real settlements.
- **Quiet losses at the Protectors** — watchtowers that go dark, garrisons that report nothing wrong right up until they stop reporting at all. The Accord does not advertise these. A crusader order that admits its watch is failing invites the exact panic it exists to prevent.
- **The slow, uncomfortable truth** that some of what gets smuggled south — including, unofficially, **[[rosvania/Wiki/Realms & Geography/Black Ice\|Black Ice]]** — moves through corrupted or compromised watch-posts. If someone inside the containment chain is dirty, it is a Silver Accord knight or officer, quietly failing the one duty that actually matters — probably telling themselves it is a small enough shipment not to matter.

## Present day — Year 3 TA

Eltharion, if he is still alive as framed, leads an order two centuries deep into a war it has never definitively won and can never fully stand down from. That is rich ground for the party: a nation defined by permanent vigilance, run by a single figure old enough to have founded it, quietly compromised from within by exactly the kind of corruption its whole purpose is to prevent.
